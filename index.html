Course Overview

Catalog Description

Critical examination of the construction of gender identity and sexuality in ancient Greece and Rome. Junior standing or higher. (Once a year) (Cross-listed as HIST 362)

Course Description

This reading-heavy course will explore the history of gender identity and sexuality in the classical, imperial eras of Greece, India, and Rome from approximately 500 BCE to 500 CE. We will cover the development of the concepts and the accompanying ethical issues of sexuality and gender and how those constructs and problematics shaped familial, social, and political structures, including their effects in other realms such as art, religion, and science. We will focus on analyzing sources by engaging in Seminar-style discussions and through presentations.

Course & Assembly Schedule

Everyone in this course is placed into groups called Assemblies. Each Assembly presents during five specific weeks across the semester, approaching the texts through their distinct analytical lens.

Assembly of Authority

Lens: institutions, law, citizenship, governance, public power, and social regulation.

Presentation Weeks:

  • Sep 8 & 10: Greek ideals of women and gender.
  • Sep 22 & 24: Greek philosophy and women’s social roles.
  • Oct 6 & 8: Augustan marriage, family, and public roles.
  • Oct 20 & 22: Women’s agency in early classical Indian law and society.
  • Nov 10 & 12: Gender, desire, and ideal womanhood in the Gupta Classical Age.

Assembly of Embodiment

Lens: lived experience, bodies, health, emotion, labor, sexuality, reproduction, disability, vulnerability, and care.

Presentation Weeks:

  • Sep 8 & 10: Greek ideals of women and gender.
  • Sep 29 & Oct 1: Female virtue in the Roman Republic.
  • Oct 13 & 15: Women’s influence in the High Roman Empire.
  • Oct 27 & 29: Marriage, devotion, and female agency in classical Indian literature.
  • Nov 10 & 12: Gender, desire, and ideal womanhood in the Gupta Classical Age.

Assembly of Relation

Lens: kinship, households, movement, exchange, migration, trade, geography, and intercultural contact.

Presentation Weeks:

  • Sep 15 & 17: Gender, sexuality, and civic life during the Peloponnesian War.
  • Sep 22 & 24: Greek philosophy and women’s social roles.
  • Oct 13 & 15: Women’s influence in the High Roman Empire.
  • Oct 27 & 29: Marriage, devotion, and female agency in classical Indian literature.
  • Nov 17 & 19: Comparing gender and sexuality across the classical world.

Assembly of Representation

Lens: literature, drama, mythology, art, religion, rhetoric, symbolism, and material culture.

Policies

Self-Evaluation Point Tracker

The Self-Evaluation Point Tracker Google Sheet is the official course record. All assignments must be submitted through this tracker. Assignments are only considered submitted with self-evaluation. No assignments are accepted outside the tracker.

Device Free Classroom

Phones, laptops, tablets, smartwatches, and other participant devices are not used during class meetings. A physical notebook and writing utensil are required to bridge individual preparation and collaborative historical work.

Email Policy

University email is official communication. When emailing the instructor, provide the course title (CLAS/HIST 362), your name, and the topic. Allow two full business days for a response.

Attendance & Participation

Attendance is required. Any unexcused missed class results in a deduction of 2 points per absence, and 1 point per late arrival or early departure. Absences are only excused via the Excused Absence Google Form with documentation. Missing any class when your Assembly is presenting results in forfeiture of all 10 Assembly Participation points for the semester.

Student Conduct & Academic Integrity

Violations of academic integrity (cheating, fabrication, tampering, plagiarism, or unauthorized AI generation) are strictly prohibited. AI usage is limited to accessibility tools (e.g., spelling/bibliography software) or supplemental troubleshooting with citations. Generating any portion of an assignment with AI and presenting it as one's own is not permitted. Violators receive a 0 on relevant assignments and likely a disciplinary record notation. Discrimination and harassment are not tolerated.

Late Work & Presentations

Speakers must submit accompanying writing by 5:00 PM the day before their presentation and bring hand-written notes; unprepared speakers will not be allowed to present. Missed presentations cannot be made up. Late writing assignments will not receive timely feedback and are not eligible for revision or regrading.
